Melursus ursinus

Physical Profile

Males are roughly 80-140 kg, females 55-95 kg; body length 1.4-1.9 m. Identified by their long shaggy black coat, heaviest around the shoulders and neck where it forms a mane. Pale cream or white V- or Y-shaped chest patch. Long pale muzzle. Curved front claws up to 8 cm, used for digging into termite mounds. The shaggy coat is unusual for a tropical bear. One interesting adaption is their the upper front incisors are missing, leaving a gap that works as a suction tube for vacuuming up insects. The nostrils can be closed voluntarily to keep out dust and insects.

Habitat and Geography

Habitsts include dry and moist tropical forest, savanna, scrubland and grassland; prefers areas with rocky outcrops and termite mounds. They're distributed across the Indian subcontinent - India, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bhutan; historically Bangladesh.

Feeding and Diet

Diet consists of termites and ants, which is unusual among bears. Also eats fruit, flowers, honey, eggs and carrion. Rips open termite mounds with the front claws, blows away the dust, then sucks insects up through the incisor gap. The sucking is loud enough to hear from a long way off. Climbs trees for fruit and honey.

Life History Cycle and Breeding Behaviors

Breeding varies by region; in India it generally mates in spring or early summer. Gestation roughly 6-7 months including delayed implantation. Litter usually 1-2 cubs, rarely 3, born in a den. Cubs ride on the mother's back from roughly 1 month until 9 months. They stay with her 2-3 years, long compared with other bears. Sexual maturity around 3 years. Lifespan roughly 20 years in the wild, up to 40 in captivity.
