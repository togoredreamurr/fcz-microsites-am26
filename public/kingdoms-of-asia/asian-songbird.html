Asian Songbird

Bali Mynah

Physical Profile

They're roughly 25 cm long and can be identified with their almost entirely white plumage with black tips on the wings and tail. Striking bare blue skin around the eye. Long drooping white crest, raised in display. Greyish legs, yellowish bill. The sexes look similar and are hard to tell apart visually, which complicates captive breeding.

Habitat and Geography

Their habitats consists of dry monsoon forests, savanna woodlands, and scrub. Distribution is endemic to the island of Bali, Indonesia, one of the smallest natural ranges of any bird. The wild population is largely confined to West Bali National Park and nearby reintroduction sites.

Feeding and Diet

The Asian Songbirds are omnivores. Diet consists of fruit, seeds, nectar, insects and other invertebrates, occasionally small reptiles. Forages in trees and on the ground, and follows large animals to catch disturbed insects.

Life History Cycle and Breeding Behaviors

Breeds in the rainy season. Monogamous, with pairs defending a territory around a nest cavity. Nests in tree hollows, sometimes old woodpecker or barbet holes. Clutch typically 2-3 eggs. Incubation roughly 12-15 days, mainly by the female. Both parents feed the chicks. Fledging at roughly 3 weeks. Captive breeding has been central to the species' survival, which links directly to the zoo's own conservation mission and makes a good closing section.
