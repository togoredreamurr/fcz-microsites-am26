Panthera tigris jacksoni

Physical Profile

One of the smallest tiger subspecies, Males can be roughly 2.4 m long and 100-130 kg; females roughly 2 m and 75-80 kg. They can be identified with their orange coat with vertical black stripes, white underside, chest and facial markings. Stripe patterns are unique to each individual, like a fingerprint, which is the basis of camera-trap population surveys. The stripes are in the skin as well as the fur.

Habitat and Geography

Habitats consist of tropical and subtropical moist broadleaf forest; lowland and hill forest. They're distributed all over the southern and central Malay Peninsula - peninsular Malaysia and the southern tip of Thailand.

Feeding and Diet

Obligate carnivore. Prey includes sambar deer, barking deer and wild boar, occasionally sun bear and young elephant. A solitarity prey hunter, stalks close, then rushes. Kills large prey with a throat bite that suffocates. Eats a very large quantity in one sitting, then goes days without feeding. Catches kills and returns to them. Playing the role of an apex predator, regulating herbivore populations, which in turn protects forest vegetation structure.

Life History Cycle and Breeding Behaviors

No fixed breeding season. Gestation roughly 103 days. Litter typically 2-3 cubs, born blind and helpless, eyes opening at about a week. They stay with the mother up to 2 years learning to hunt. Females raise cubs alone. Sexual maturity around 3-4 years for females, 4-5 for males. Lifespan roughly 15-20 years in the wild, longer under human care.
